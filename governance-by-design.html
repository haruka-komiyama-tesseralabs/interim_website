Governance by design: keeping AI agents auditable

By Marcus Delgado · March 18, 2026 at 10:40 AM

Enterprises running regulated workloads on SAP don't get to adopt an AI system on faith. Every change to a financial or operational system of record needs to be explainable after the fact, which means the bar for an AI agent operating in that environment isn't just "does it work," it's "can we prove what it did and why."

We built Tessera's governance layer around that constraint from the start rather than bolting it on afterward. Every action an agent proposes is logged with the reasoning behind it, the inputs it considered, and the exact diff it would apply, before any of it touches a live system. Nothing executes unilaterally past a defined checkpoint — agents propose, and a human with the right context approves.

This is a meaningfully different model from a black-box automation tool that just produces an output. The audit trail isn't a log tacked onto the side of the system; it's the same record a reviewer uses to approve the change in the first place, which means the explanation always matches what actually happened.

For customers operating under SOX or sector-specific compliance regimes, that trail is exportable as a report their auditors can review directly, without needing to take our word for what the agents did.

We're expanding this tooling further this year, including finer-grained approval scopes and better tooling for compliance teams to define what "safe to auto-approve" means for their own environment, rather than inheriting a one-size-fits-all default.
